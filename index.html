Deliverable 4 — Draft Analytical Report

Due: Month 3 · Status: DRAFT · ILLUSTRATIVE NARRATIVE — values pending microdata

Executive Summary

This report presents the analysis of the 2022 Saint Lucia Enterprise Census. It describes the structure of Saint Lucia's business sector, identifies policy-relevant findings, and — where classifications permit — examines changes since the 2009 Enterprise Census. The report is organized into six chapters: context and methodology; enterprise characteristics; economic activity and industry (ISIC Rev.4); employment; geographic distribution; and comparability with 2009. Policy implications and recommendations conclude each chapter.

Draft status. Numerical findings will be inserted from the validated CSO microdata. The narrative structure, analytical framing, and policy-relevance sections are final.

Chapter 1 — Context and Methodology

Saint Lucia's enterprise sector is dominated by micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs), a structure common across the OECS. The 2022 Enterprise Census provides the most comprehensive recent enumeration of enterprises, their characteristics, and their employment. This chapter sets out the census scope, the ISIC Rev.4 classification applied, the data validation steps (per D2), and the comparability caveats vis-à-vis 2009.

1.1 Methodological Note

Where 2009 and 2022 classifications differ (notably ISIC Rev.3→Rev.4), comparisons are presented only for mappable categories, with correspondence tables documented in Annex A. Non-mappable comparisons are explicitly excluded and explained.

Chapter 2 — Enterprise Characteristics

Key finding (illustrative): Micro-enterprises (1–4 employees) account for the majority of enterprises, consistent with the MSME-dominated structure of Saint Lucia's private sector. PLACEHOLDER

2.1 Size Distribution

See Table T1.1. The size distribution is heavily right-skewed: a small number of large enterprises account for a disproportionate share of employment, while the enterprise count is dominated by micro and small firms.

2.2 Ownership

Ownership categories (sole proprietorship, partnership, limited company, other) are analyzed with a "Not stated" category retained to preserve data integrity (per D2 issue log).

Policy Implications

  • MSME-targeted support (access to finance, business development services) remains the primary lever for enterprise growth.
  • Data gaps in ownership suggest a case for improved future census coverage of legal-status variables.

Chapter 3 — Economic Activity and Industry (ISIC Rev.4)

Key finding (illustrative): Wholesale/retail trade (Section G) and accommodation/food services (Section I) are expected to be the largest sectors by enterprise count, reflecting tourism and domestic commerce. PLACEHOLDER

3.1 Industry Composition

Enterprise counts and employment are reported by ISIC Rev.4 section (A–U), with sub-section detail for the largest sectors. Concentration indices (e.g., HHI) are computed to characterize sectoral diversity.

Policy Implications

  • Tourism-linked sectors (I) and trade (G) warrant targeted resilience and productivity programs.
  • Sectoral diversification policy should be informed by the concentration analysis.

Chapter 4 — Employment

Key finding (illustrative): Female employment share is expected to differ materially by sector, with higher shares in services and lower shares in construction and manufacturing. PLACEHOLDER

4.1 Employment by Sex

Sex-disaggregated employment is analyzed by size class, ISIC section, and geography (Tables T2.3–T2.5). Missing sex data (2.1%, per D2) is reported as a distinct category and not imputed.

4.2 Employment by Size

Mean employees per enterprise rises with size class; within-class variance is reported to capture heterogeneity among large firms.

Policy Implications

  • Gender-responsive enterprise policy should target sectors with low female representation and support female-led MSMEs in high-growth services.
  • Large-firm employment concentration raises labour-market resilience questions.

Chapter 5 — Geographic Distribution

Key finding (illustrative): Enterprise activity concentrates in the Castries and Gros Islet corridors, consistent with population distribution (Gros Islet 24,435; Castries North 10,530; Castries East 10,286 per the 2022 Population Census). PLACEHOLDER for enterprise values

5.1 Constituency-Level Analysis

Enterprises and employment are mapped to constituencies. Maps are produced to CSO publication standards. Per-capita context uses the real 2022 Population Census figures (total 171,834) to compute enterprises per 1,000 population.

Policy Implications

  • Regional enterprise support should reflect the north-south activity gradient.
  • Rural constituencies may require differentiated MSME support.

Chapter 6 — Comparability with the 2009 Enterprise Census

Where classifications permit, changes since 2009 are examined. ISIC Rev.3→Rev.4 mappings are applied using UN correspondence tables; only mappable categories are compared. PLACEHOLDER for change values

6.1 Caveats

Differences in coverage, concepts, reference periods, and classifications are documented in Annex A. Comparisons are presented with explicit caveats and are not used for headline growth claims where mappings are uncertain.

Chapter 7 — Conclusions and Recommendations

  1. MSME focus: Policy should prioritize micro and small enterprise productivity and access to finance.
  2. Sectoral resilience: Tourism-linked and trade sectors require targeted resilience programs.
  3. Gender: Gender-responsive enterprise policy should address sectoral segregation in employment.
  4. Geography: Differentiated regional support reflecting the north-south gradient.
  5. Data: Future censuses should improve coverage of ownership and sex-disaggregated employment to reduce non-response.

3. Reproducible Code Package

The analysis is delivered as a fully reproducible pipeline. The Python module below computes the key tables; an equivalent SPSS syntax file is provided. Both operate on the CSO-approved microdata (never leaving the secure environment).

3.1 Python analysis pipeline (excerpt)

# slu_enterprise_census_analysis.py # Reproducible analysis pipeline — Saint Lucia Enterprise Census 2022 # Author: AI Statistician × DeepSeek (Lead Architect) import pandas as pd import numpy as np import json # 1. Load CSO-approved microdata (secure environment only) df = pd.read_csv("slu_enterprise_census_2022.csv") # 2. Size-class derivation (employment bands) def size_class(n): if n <= 4: return "Micro" elif n <= 19: return "Small" elif n <= 99: return "Medium" else: return "Large" df["size_class"] = df["employment_total"].apply(size_class) # 3. Table T1.1 — Enterprises by size class (weighted) t11 = (df.groupby("size_class") .apply(lambda x: np.average(x["count"], weights=x["census_weight"])) .rename("frequency") .reset_index()) t11["pct"] = (t11["frequency"] / t11["frequency"].sum() * 100).round(1) # 4. Table T2.3 — Employment by sex (weighted) t23 = (df.groupby("sex") .apply(lambda x: np.average(x["employment_total"], weights=x["census_weight"])) .rename("employment") .reset_index()) t23["pct"] = (t23["employment"] / t23["employment"].sum() * 100).round(1) # 5. Table T2.4 — Employment by sex × ISIC section t24 = (df.groupby(["sex", "isic_section"]) .apply(lambda x: np.average(x["employment_total"], weights=x["census_weight"])) .unstack(fill_value=0) .round(0)) # 6. Export tables as JSON (machine-readable) and CSV with open("outputs_tables.json", "w") as f: json.dump({ "T1.1": t11.to_dict(orient="records"), "T2.3": t23.to_dict(orient="records"), "T2.4": t24.to_dict(), }, f, indent=2) t11.to_csv("T1.1_enterprises_by_size.csv", index=False) t23.to_csv("T2.3_employment_by_sex.csv", index=False) print("Analysis complete. Outputs written.")

3.2 SPSS syntax (equivalent, for CSO analysts)

* Saint Lucia Enterprise Census 2022 — SPSS syntax. * Author: AI Statistician (SPSS design expertise) × DeepSeek. * 1. Load data (secure environment). GET FILE='/secure/slu_enterprise_census_2022.sav'. * 2. Size class derivation. COMPUTE size_class = 1. IF (employment_total <= 4) size_class = 1. IF (employment_total >= 5 AND employment_total <= 19) size_class = 2. IF (employment_total >= 20 AND employment_total <= 99) size_class = 3. IF (employment_total >= 100) size_class = 4. VALUE LABELS size_class 1 'Micro' 2 'Small' 3 'Medium' 4 'Large'. EXECUTE. * 3. Table T1.1 — Enterprises by size class (weighted). WEIGHT BY census_weight. FREQUENCIES VARIABLES=size_class /ORDER=ANALYSIS. * 4. Table T2.3 — Employment by sex. MEANS TABLES=employment_total BY sex /CELLS=MEAN SUM COUNT. * 5. Table T2.4 — Employment by sex × ISIC. CROSSTABS /TABLES=sex BY isic_section /CELLS=COUNT ROW COLUMN /STATISTICS=CHISQ. * 6. Comparability (2009↔2022) — only on mappable categories. * (Mapping applied via correspondence table — see Annex A.)
